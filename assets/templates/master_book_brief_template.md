# Master Book Brief

Fill in whatever you already know and leave the rest blank — nothing here is required
before pasting this into a session to start a new book. Anything left blank gets proposed
by Claude from what you *did* fill in, and confirmed with you before anything is built;
anything you fill in gets used as stated, not second-guessed. This form exists so you can
prepare a book's setup once, offline, and hand it over in one paste instead of a live
back-and-forth — see `references/project_setup.md`'s intake-from-brief procedure.

If this book joins an existing imprint that already has an `Imprint_Profile.md` (logo,
standard taglines, category colour coding), skip the Imprint section below — say which
imprint it joins instead, and those settings carry over rather than being re-collected.

---

## 1. Working Title and Subtitle

Title:
Subtitle (if any):

## 2. Thesis

The central argument, in one sentence — what is this book trying to prove?

## 3. Target of Critique

What ideas, doctrines, institutions, policies, movements, or public actions does the book
argue for or against?

## 4. Named People

Any identifiable individuals whose public conduct or statements the book will discuss.
(This is an inventory for risk-tracking, not a legal assessment.)

## 5. Register and Voice

Scholarly, journalistic, polemical-but-rigorous, something else? If you want the book to
read in a specific recognizable nonfiction mode rather than your own natural prose (e.g.
"sweeping macro-history," or a comparable writer as a touchstone), say so here instead of
in section 16.

## 6. Audience

Who is this written for?

## 7. Target Length

Approximate word count, chapter count, or both.

## 8. Chapter Spine

A working list of chapters or topics, in order, grouped into Parts if you already have
them in mind. This is a starting point, not a locked outline — leave blank and Claude will
propose one from your thesis and target of critique.

## 9. Known Flashpoints

Which contested, controversial, or high-scrutiny claims do you already know the book will
touch?

## 10. Genre Traps

Categories of claim this book will need extra scrutiny on. Leave blank and Claude will
propose from sections 2, 3, and 9 — the generic set is disputed quotes, current law and its
status, contested history, position overgeneralization, statistics, causal claims, and
motive/state-of-mind claims about named people, plus this book's own specifics (named
cases, statutes, organizations, disputed events, contested studies).

## 11. Citation Style

Chicago, Bluebook, a house style, or "preserve an existing scheme" if you're migrating an
already-written manuscript that has its own citation ID format in place.

## 12. Standalone or Series

Standalone, or: series name, whether this is book one, and — if the series already
exists — where the current `Source_Registry.md` and series outline live.

## 13. Imprint and Required Reviewers

Publisher/imprint name (or "self-published"), and which subject-matter disciplines must
review before publication (legal, clinical, historical, etc. — whatever the book's claims
touch). Leave the reviewer list blank and Claude will propose one from the subject matter.

## 14. Existing Research or Draft Material

Do you already have a folder of sources, prior notes, an existing bibliography, or a
partial or complete draft? Say what and roughly how much — this changes how the first
chapter gets opened.

## 15. Open Questions

Anything you already know you'll want a human expert or lawyer to weigh in on before this
publishes.

---

## 16. Voice Sample (optional)

Paste one or two samples of your own prose here (a published piece, a blog post, a chapter
from a previous book) if you want the voice pass to target your actual writing rather than
a named register from section 5. Skip this if section 5 already named a target register,
or if you'd rather Claude build the voice profile from your approved drafts as the book
progresses.

## 17. Core Definitions (optional)

Any key terms the book relies on that you already want defined precisely and consistently.
Leave blank and Claude will draft this from the rest of the brief for you to confirm.

## 18. Core Distinctions (optional)

Any pairs of concepts the book needs to keep carefully separate — often the book's real
intellectual contribution. Leave blank and Claude will draft this from the rest of the
brief for you to confirm.

## 19. Anything Else

Whatever doesn't fit above: house style rules, approved recurring phrases, a working book
code for claim IDs, trim size preference, or anything else worth knowing before setup
starts.
