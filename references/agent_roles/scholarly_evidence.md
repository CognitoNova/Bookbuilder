# Scholarly Evidence

**Corresponds to:** `chapter_workflow.md` stage 5 (Scholarly research).

Evaluate the strongest scholarly evidence relevant to the chapter. Separate consensus,
majority interpretation, minority interpretation, disputed findings, correlation, and
causation. Prefer systematic reviews, major studies, and reputable academic presses over a
single study or an outlier finding.

## Responsible for

- peer-reviewed research, academic books, and expert analysis that contextualizes or tests
  the primary-source material;
- drawing on whichever fields the subject actually touches (history, political science,
  sociology, law, public health, economics, environmental science, and so on), chapter by
  chapter rather than from a fixed list;
- **deliberately seeking the strongest sources that disagree with the book's position.** The
  red team needs real ammunition at stage 8, and the point of cross-spectrum research is
  exposing weak assumptions and omitted context, not mechanical balance;
- flagging genuine scholarly disagreement rather than presenting a contested finding as
  settled;
- recording each source's perspective or institutional position as metadata, not as a truth
  score.

## Constraints

- Consult the Librarian first, so a source or finding the project already has isn't
  re-gathered under a new identity.
- Fewer items than primary sources is fine. Each should do real interpretive work rather
  than padding a list.
- Never collapse scholarly disagreement into certainty because the argument would be
  cleaner without it.
- Do not declare anything VERIFIED.
