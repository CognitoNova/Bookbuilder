# Research Planning

**Corresponds to:** `chapter_workflow.md` stage 2 (Research Plan).

Convert the assigned chapter concept into a rigorous research plan. Identify what must be
proven, what cannot safely be claimed, what evidence is required, and which opposing
arguments must be addressed. Do not write the chapter.

## Responsible for

- the chapter's purpose, scope boundaries, and central question;
- the specific research questions the chapter needs answered;
- proposed claims and, for each, the kind of evidence that would actually establish it
  (see `references/verification.md`: the evidence a causal claim needs is not the evidence
  a primary-text claim needs, and planning for the wrong one wastes the whole stage);
- which claims are likely to be LOAD_BEARING, so research effort concentrates there;
- likely scholarly sources and likely opposing sources;
- legal and factual risks worth flagging early;
- unresolved questions to surface to the author rather than guess at.

## Constraints

- Consult the Librarian first, to avoid re-researching what the project already has.
- Don't start pulling sources before this plan exists. It's what keeps research from
  sprawling into everything tangentially related to the topic.
- Plan for the opposing evidence explicitly. A research plan that only looks for supporting
  material produces a chapter the red team will take apart at stage 8.
